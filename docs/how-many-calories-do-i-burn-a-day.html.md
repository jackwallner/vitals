## How Many Calories Do I Burn a Day?

By Jack Wallner · Last updated October 25, 2026

Most adults burn between 1,600 and 2,400 calories a day if they are women and 2,000 to 3,000 if they are men, according to the Dietary Guidelines for Americans. Total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) is a total that adds up your resting metabolism, digestion, everyday movement and exercise. Your own number depends on your size, age and how much you move. The largest share is resting metabolism, which you burn even when doing nothing. An Apple Watch can measure your real total by adding active and resting energy.

General information, not medical advice. Calorie estimates are population averages. Talk to a clinician before changing your diet if you have a medical condition or are pregnant.

## What makes up your daily calorie burn?

Your daily burn has four parts: resting metabolism, digestion, everyday movement and deliberate exercise. Most of it is your resting metabolism, the energy your body spends just keeping you alive. The rest is digestion, everyday movement such as walking and fidgeting, and deliberate exercise. Nutrition researchers call the total **TDEE**, total daily energy expenditure. If your weight is steady, the calories you eat each day are roughly equal to it.

## How many calories does the average person burn per day by age and sex?

Adult women burn about 1,600 to 2,400 calories a day and adult men about 2,000 to 3,000, depending on age and activity. The Dietary Guidelines for Americans (2020-2025 edition, Appendix 2) publish estimated daily calorie needs for adults to maintain weight. The figures assume a reference man who is 5 ft 10 in and 154 lb, and a reference woman who is 5 ft 4 in and 126 lb. Across all adult ages, the range is 1,600 to 2,400 calories for women and 2,000 to 3,000 for men.

| Age | Men: sedentary / moderate / active | Women: sedentary / moderate / active |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 21 to 25 | 2,400 / 2,800 / 3,000 | 2,000 / 2,200 / 2,400 |
| 31 to 35 | 2,400 / 2,600 / 3,000 | 1,800 / 2,000 / 2,200 |
| 41 to 45 | 2,200 / 2,600 / 2,800 | 1,800 / 2,000 / 2,200 |
| 51 to 55 | 2,200 / 2,400 / 2,800 | 1,600 / 1,800 / 2,200 |
| 61 to 65 | 2,000 / 2,400 / 2,600 | 1,600 / 1,800 / 2,000 |
| 76 and up | 2,000 / 2,200 / 2,400 | 1,600 / 1,800 / 2,000 |

The activity levels are narrower than they sound. *Sedentary* means only the activity of independent living. *Moderately active* adds the equivalent of walking about 1.5 to 3 miles a day at 3 to 4 miles an hour. *Active* is more than 3 miles a day. The Guidelines note that needs generally fall with age because basal metabolic rate declines, and that these are only estimates.

## How many calories do you burn doing nothing?

The resting part is your basal metabolic rate (BMR). The Mifflin-St Jeor equation, published in 1990 in the *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, estimates it from weight, height and age:

- Men: 10 x kg + 6.25 x cm - 5 x age + 5
- Women: 10 x kg + 6.25 x cm - 5 x age - 161

Worked example: the reference woman above (57.2 kg, 162.6 cm) at age 35 comes to about 1,250 calories a day at rest. The reference man (69.9 kg, 177.8 cm) at 35 comes to about 1,640. That is roughly 52 and 68 calories an hour of lying still. Both are below the sedentary figures in the table, because even a sedentary day includes digestion and getting around.

## Why does your number differ from the average?

Your own number depends on your size, age and how much you move.

- **Size.** Bigger bodies burn more, which is why the formula uses weight and height.
- **Age.** The Guidelines tie falling needs in adulthood to a lower BMR.
- **Movement you do not count as exercise.** Steps, standing and chores shift the total. A person who walks 14,000 steps a day and one who walks 4,000 can pick the same activity level and burn different amounts.
- **Method.** The formula gave about 1,250 at rest for a reference woman, while the Guidelines table gave 1,800 for a sedentary 35-year-old. Different methods land a few hundred calories apart.

The NIH's Body Weight Planner, from NIDDK, takes your age, sex, height, weight and activity level and returns the calories to maintain your weight. It cautions that its estimate may run high for people with a very sedentary lifestyle.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many calories does the average person burn a day?

About 1,600 to 2,400 calories for adult women and 2,000 to 3,000 for adult men. That is what the Dietary Guidelines for Americans estimate adults need to maintain weight, depending on age and activity. These are estimates for reference-size adults.

### How many calories do I burn doing nothing?

Roughly 1,250 to 1,640 calories a day for reference-size adults, your basal metabolic rate. The Mifflin-St Jeor equation gives about 1,250 calories a day for a 35-year-old reference woman and about 1,640 for a 35-year-old reference man, or roughly 52 and 68 calories an hour.

### Why does my Apple Watch show fewer calories than these averages?

Because the Move ring and the Watch's headline number count only active energy. Your total burn is active energy plus resting energy, both of which the Watch records in Apple Health.

### Do you burn fewer calories as you get older?

Generally yes. The Dietary Guidelines say adult calorie needs tend to decrease with age because basal metabolic rate declines.

### How accurate are calorie burn estimates?

They are approximations. The Dietary Guidelines call their figures only estimates, and different methods can differ by a few hundred calories. A measured average over several weeks reflects you better than a formula.
