By Jack Wallner · Last updated October 25, 2026

Best for a one-time estimate from your body stats: TDEE.** A calorie burn tracker is an app that reads what your Apple Watch measured, while a TDEE calculator estimates from age, height, weight and activity. ## The apps, one by one

### Total Calories

Total Calories reads active and resting energy from Apple Health, adds them and shows one daily total on the Apple Watch face, the Home Screen, the Lock Screen and an iPhone dashboard. Watch complications and widgets are free, and it needs no account. Limits: it is not a food logger, the free tier is the burn side only, the figure is whatever your Watch and iPhone record (there is no formula estimate from body stats), and the iPhone dashboard has no hour-by-hour view. It has only 11 ratings so far.

### ActivityTracker Pedometer

ActivityTracker is the established choice. Its listing describes steps, active calories, distance, active time and floors, an hourly view, weekly targets split into daily ones, GPS activities with route maps, and an Apple Watch app. Calorie targets, import and export and some other features are marked PRO. The calorie figure it describes is active calories, so look elsewhere if you want resting energy included in one number.

### TDEE (Apperitif)

This is a calculator, not a tracker. You enter sex, age, height, weight and activity level and get a maintenance figure, a table of deficit and surplus targets, BMI, and a macro split. It reads nothing from Apple Health and keeps no history, so there is nothing to come back to after the first use. It is fast and the core calculator was free in our test, with Pro covering customization and some inputs.

### TDEE Calculator (Clixade)

The listing describes TDEE, BMR, RMR, BMI and lean mass outputs, plus percentage deficit and surplus targets and a body fat input. It also lists AI-generated content with an accuracy disclaimer. In our test a plan screen appeared before the first result, so check what is free before you enter your details. It does not read Apple Health.

### Dynamic TDEE Tracker

This one estimates TDEE backward from what you eat and how your weight moves over 12 weeks, instead of from a formula or a Watch. You log calories daily and weigh in, or pull weight from Apple Health. That is a different method, and a good one if you already log food, but it shows little on day one. It has only 4 ratings, so its score is unproven.

## Which should you pick?

If you wear an Apple Watch and want active plus resting calories on your watch face, pick Total Calories. Without a watch, use a TDEE calculator.

- You wear an Apple Watch and want your whole-day burn, active plus resting, on the watch face: [Total Calories](https://jackwallner.com/ios/vitals/).
- You want hourly breakdowns, step targets or GPS workouts in one place: ActivityTracker Pedometer.
- You have no watch and want a quick estimate from your body stats: TDEE or TDEE Calculator. Compare what is free before you type your details.
- You log food every day and want maintenance calories inferred from your own weight trend: Dynamic TDEE Tracker.
- You already use a food logger and want to see burn next to intake: Total Calories shows a net figure from the dietary energy that logger writes to Apple Health, in its paid tier.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best app to track calories burned on Apple Watch?

Total Calories shows total daily burn (active plus resting) on the watch face. ActivityTracker Pedometer gives hourly detail and workouts. Both read data your Apple Watch records in Apple Health.

### Is there a way to track calories burned on an Apple Watch?

Yes. The Watch records active energy and resting energy in Apple Health. The Move ring counts only active energy, so a third-party app is useful if you want the combined total on a watch face.

### Are TDEE calculator apps the same as calorie burn trackers?

No. A calculator estimates your daily burn from age, height, weight and activity level. A tracker reads what your devices measured. The two numbers often differ by a few hundred calories.

### Do I need a food logger to use Total Calories?

No. Total Calories shows burn only. If you want intake next to it, a separate food logger can write dietary energy to Apple Health and the paid tier will show the difference.

### Is this comparison independent?

Not entirely. Jack Wallner makes Total Calories. Competitor entries use their public App Store listings and our own testing, and the listings are linked below so you can check them.

### Is the Apple Watch Fitness app accurate for calories burned?

It is an estimate, not a lab measurement. Apple says the Watch uses your height, weight, sex and age to calculate calories, and that a snug fit and calibrating the Watch improve accuracy, so keep your Health details current. Also note that the Move ring in the Fitness app counts active calories only, so it reads well below your full daily burn until you add resting energy.

### Can MyFitnessPal track calories burned on Apple Watch?

Partly. MyFitnessPal is a food logger first, built around logging what you eat against a daily calorie goal. Per its help center, its Apple Watch app shows calories remaining, lets you quick-add calories and water, and can use your step data to raise your daily calorie goal, and Apple Watch workouts sync into its diary once Apple Health is linked. The same help center says it does not yet factor in the active calories your watch measures separately, so its total may not match your watch's burn. Food loggers such as MyFitnessPal and FatSecret answer how much you ate. If you want how much you burned, active plus resting, use a tracker that reads both from Apple Health, such as Total Calories, alongside your food logger.
