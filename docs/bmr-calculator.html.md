## BMR calculator and what resting burn really is

Basal metabolic rate is the energy your body spends just staying alive: breathing, circulation, cell repair, keeping warm. It is the largest share of what you burn each day.

## What is BMR?

BMR is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest, 1,200 to 2,000 kcal a day for a typical adult. BMR is measured at complete rest, fasted, in a neutral-temperature room. It covers the work your organs do with no movement at all. For a typical adult that is 1,200 to 2,000 kcal a day, and it accounts for 60 to 70% of total daily burn in anyone who is not an athlete.

## What formula does the BMR calculator use?

The calculator uses Mifflin-St Jeor, which outperformed the older Harris-Benedict equation in validation studies and is the one most dietitians use.

BMR (men) = 10 x kg + 6.25 x cm - 5 x age + 5
BMR (women) = 10 x kg + 6.25 x cm - 5 x age - 161

## What changes your BMR?

Lean mass, age and body size set most of your BMR, and sleep, illness and heat move it a few percent.

- **Lean mass.** Muscle burns more at rest than fat. Two people at the same weight can differ by a few hundred calories.
- **Age.** BMR drifts down roughly 1 to 2% per decade after your twenties, mostly from lost muscle.
- **Size.** A bigger body has more tissue to maintain, so BMR rises with weight and height.
- **Sleep, illness and heat.** Short-term swings of a few percent are normal.

## How does BMR compare with Apple Watch resting energy?

Apple Health has a **resting energy** field that is the Watch's own estimate of your BMR, built from your height, weight, age and sex in the Health profile and spread across the day. It is close to the Mifflin-St Jeor number but updates as your profile changes, and because it lands in Health hour by hour you can see how much of today's burn is resting versus active.

Total Calories shows that split on its dashboard and adds the two halves into one daily total, which is the number most people actually want when they ask about their BMR: not the resting figure alone, but what it means for how much they can eat.

## How do you get from BMR to a calorie target?

Multiply BMR by an activity factor, or better, read your measured daily total, and set a deficit or surplus from there. BMR is a floor, not a target. Eating below it for long is neither necessary nor pleasant. The [TDEE guide](https://jackwallner.com/ios/vitals/tdee-calculator.html) covers that step.

## Common questions

### Is BMR the same as resting metabolic rate?

Nearly. RMR is measured under slightly looser conditions and runs about 10% higher than true BMR. Fitness apps, including Apple Health's resting energy, use the two interchangeably.

### Should I eat my BMR to lose weight?

No. BMR is the energy you burn at complete rest. Your real daily burn is higher, so set a moderate deficit from your total daily expenditure, not from BMR.

### Why is my Apple Watch resting energy different from the calculator?

Because Apple uses its own model and the height, weight, age and sex in your Health profile. Small differences of 50 to 150 kcal are expected. Keep your Health profile current and the number tracks you better than any formula.
